# Ion Exchange Water Softening — Lab Experiment

A concise guide for an undergraduate chemistry experiment to **remove water hardness** using a **cation-exchange column** and measure total hardness by **EDTA titration**.

---

## Overview
This repository provides instructions and supporting files for a laboratory experiment demonstrating:
- **Water Softening**: Replacing Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions with Na⁺ using a cation-exchange resin.
- **Hardness Measurement**: Quantifying total hardness in ppm (as CaCO₃) via EDTA titration.

---

## Aim
Soften hard water and determine its total hardness in parts per million (ppm) expressed as CaCO₃.

---

## Theory
- Hard water contains **Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺** ions.
- A sodium-form cation-exchange resin swaps Na⁺ for these hardness ions.
- **EDTA Titration**: Uses Eriochrome Black-T (EBT) indicator and an ammonia buffer (pH 9–10). The color change from **wine-red to blue** marks the endpoint.

---

## Materials
- Cation-exchange column/resin  
- Standard 0.01 M EDTA solution  
- Ammonia/ammonium chloride buffer (pH 9–10)  
- Eriochrome Black-T indicator  
- Burette, conical flask, measuring cylinder

---

## Procedure
1. Pack the column with Na⁺-form cation-exchange resin.  
2. Pass a measured volume of tap water through the column to soften it.  
3. Titrate 100 mL of the treated water with 0.01 M EDTA using EBT indicator and buffer.  
4. Record EDTA volume at the wine-red → blue endpoint.  
5. Repeat for accuracy and calculate hardness (mg/L as CaCO₃).

---

## Results (Example)
- **Average EDTA used:** ~9.3 mL  
- **Calculated Hardness:** **~93 ppm (as CaCO₃)**
